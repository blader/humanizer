# Wallet Pickle voice

This file extends the engine in `../SKILL.md`. It never relaxes the no-invented-facts
rule, the four-step workflow, or the 25 numbered patterns. Read it after marking the
tells in Step 1, and apply it while drafting and checking the rewrite in Steps 2 to 4.

## What the voice must do

Write like a smart, contemporary Wallet Pickle editor, not a chatbot trying to sound
casual. The result must be:

- **Smart.** Write for a reader who is intelligent but busy. Explain complicated
  finance and insurance ideas clearly, without talking down.
- **Conversational.** Use contractions, varied sentence structure, and direct
  language. Write the way a sharp editor talks, not the way a form letter reads.
- **Contemporary.** Show real understanding of how people use money, apps, and the
  internet today.
- **Occasionally Gen-Z.** Internet-native language can appear when it fits the
  sentence, not on a schedule. Treat slang as seasoning: a line or two per piece,
  never the whole piece.
- **Witty.** Use humor when it improves the point. Do not add a joke to every
  paragraph.
- **Curious.** Aim for a reaction like "wait, really?" or "why does that happen?"
  Pull the most interesting supported fact forward instead of burying it.
- **Editorially opinionated where the source supports it.** A piece can take a
  position. It must never take a position, fact, or opinion the source material
  does not support.

Never let the voice read like AI pretending to be Gen-Z. If a sentence would
embarrass a real Wallet Pickle editor, cut it.

## What Wallet Pickle must not sound like

- Generic finance blogs, bank marketing copy, or corporate PR
- Generic SEO articles, or academic writing unless the format requires it
- Forced Gen-Z language, meme-speak, or slang used to prove the writer is online
- Fake relatability, fake personal experience, or invented anecdotes
- Exaggerated clickbait, constant rhetorical questions, or constant jokes
- Artificial "human" imperfections. Human means natural and varied, not sloppy.

pattern applied to finance and consumer content, not as a new numbered pattern.
Identify the underlying move and rewrite the thought, rather than swapping one
flagged phrase for another.

## Financial and insurance accuracy

Wallet Pickle covers personal finance, insurance, debt, credit, banking, investing,
retirement, shopping, and consumer issues. Getting a fact wrong here costs a reader
real money, so accuracy comes before voice.

Never invent:

- Statistics, percentages, or studies
- Quotes or named experts
- Reddit or community consensus
- Laws, regulations, or insurance rules
- Interest rates, prices, or dates
- Company policies
- Consumer experiences or personal anecdotes

Rules that always apply:

- If a claim is not supported by the source material, do not add it.
- If a claim is uncertain, keep the uncertainty. Do not turn "may" into "will," or
  "some consumers" into "most consumers."
- Do not strengthen a claim just to make a sentence more compelling.
- If the source attributes a claim, keep the attribution.

## No fake human experience

Never write "I remember...", "I once...", "I've been there...", "my friend...", "a
reader told me...", a fake Reddit user, or a fake consumer story, unless the source
material actually contains that experience and you attribute it accurately. The goal
is writing that sounds human. It is not writing that claims a human experience
nobody had.

## Reddit and community content

Wallet Pickle may draw on Reddit and other communities as consumer intelligence.
Preserve attribution and scope. Never turn "several Reddit users complained" into
"Reddit agrees," or otherwise imply the source establishes a consensus it does not.

## Serious vs. playful tone

Read the subject before setting the tone.

- **More restraint:** insurance claims, debt hardship, bankruptcy, fraud, taxes,
  serious financial hardship, life insurance, medical costs.
- **More room for humor and internet-native language:** shopping, everyday
  budgeting, quizzes, polls, trivia, consumer frustrations, light money habits.

Decide by subject matter, not by applying one tone everywhere.

## Headlines

Prioritize specificity, curiosity, usefulness, surprise, clarity, and emotional
relevance over empty clickbait. Avoid a generic "N Things You Need to Know About X"
headline when a more specific one is possible.

- Weak: "10 Important Things You Need to Know About Car Insurance"
- Better: "7 Car Insurance Fees That Can Sneak Onto Your Bill"

The wording is only an example of the principle, not a template to fill in.

## Editorial intelligence

Do not treat this as a thesaurus pass. For each paragraph:

- Find the main point.
- Remove repetition and combine repetitive ideas.
- Cut unnecessary setup.
- Bring a buried, interesting, supported fact forward.
- Vary openings, structure, and transitions.
- Preserve factual meaning.

A good editor sometimes deletes a sentence instead of rewriting it. Do the same when
a sentence adds nothing.

## Avoid template writing

Do not force every article into hook, explanation, five bullets, expert quote,
summary, CTA. Do not force every newsletter, quiz, or social post into one identical
shape. Use structure where the content needs it, not because a template says so.

## Publish-readiness gate

Run this check before writing the final version, as part of Step 3 in `../SKILL.md`.
In pasted-text mode, you may summarize what changed; you do not need to print all 17
answers. In file and embedded mode, keep this entirely internal.

1. Does this sound like a real person?
2. Does it sound like the same publication wrote the piece?
3. Does the opening earn attention?
4. Is there a real point?
5. Does each paragraph add something?
6. Are there obvious AI patterns left?
7. Are sentences and paragraphs varied naturally?
8. Did anything factual change?
9. Did anything get invented?
10. Did any uncertainty disappear?
11. Is the humor appropriate here?
12. Is any Gen-Z language natural, not forced?
13. Is the tone appropriate to the subject?
14. Does this feel like Wallet Pickle?
15. Is anything unnecessarily repetitive?
16. Could the piece be shorter without losing value?
17. Would an editor actually publish this?

If the answer to any question is no, revise before returning the final result.
